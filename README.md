# Synthetic Auto Pricing and Portfolio Impact Lab

An end-to-end insurance pricing analytics project built to demonstrate the methods in a Data Scientist II role: framing a pricing question, building frequency and severity models, validating out of time, checking calibration, measuring segment-level portfolio impact, and documenting model limitations.

> **Data note:** All policy and claim records are generated by this project. They are synthetic and do not represent an insurer, customers, or real premium indications.

## Business question

Can a simple, interpretable frequency-severity model produce better calibrated relative risk estimates than a flat rate, and how would an illustrative capped indication vary across portfolio segments?

The workflow deliberately distinguishes risk estimation from a filed or deployable price. Expense and margin load, current premium, and rate-change caps are teaching assumptionsâ€”not actuarial guidance or approved business economics.


The pipeline creates a reproducible synthetic portfolio, trains on policy years 2021â€“2023, holds out 2024, and writes diagnostics to `reports/`. The generated policy-level file is ignored by Git; summary CSVs, metrics, and PNGs can be shared.

## Method

1. Generate policy-year exposures, categorical rating factors, claim counts, claim costs, and a noisy synthetic current premium.
2. Fit a Poisson regression to claim frequency, using exposure as the sample weight.
3. Fit a Gamma regression to mean claim severity, weighting policy averages by claim count.
4. Multiply predicted annual frequency and severity to obtain expected pure premium.
5. Evaluate once on the latest policy year, with Poisson and Gamma deviance plus pure-premium MAE/RMSE.
6. Review observed-versus-expected calibration by risk decile and compare illustrative capped indications by territory, vehicle type, and age band.

## Outputs

- `reports/model_metrics.json`: holdout scope and evaluation metrics.
- `reports/calibration_by_risk_decile.csv`: observed and expected frequency, severity, and pure premium by decile.
- `reports/portfolio_segment_impact.csv`: observed loss cost and capped illustrative premium changes by segment.
- `reports/graphs/pure_premium_calibration.png`: observed versus expected loss cost across predicted-risk deciles.
- `reports/graphs/observed_expected_by_decile.png`: calibration ratio by decile.
- `reports/graphs/territory_rate_impact.png`: illustrative capped change by territory.

## How this maps to the role

- **Insurance pricing and segmentation:** separate frequency and severity GLM-style models, pure-premium indications, and segment summaries.
- **Scientific validation:** chronological holdout, exposure-aware frequency fitting, appropriate deviance metrics, and calibration review.
- **Portfolio impact:** capped indication scenarios that show trade-offs at segment level.
- **Production-quality practice:** small modules, deterministic synthetic data, typed interfaces, documented assumptions, and focused unit tests.
- **Communication:** metrics and chart labels explicitly identify synthetic data, out-of-time validation, and illustrative assumptions.

## Limitations and next steps

This is a demonstration, not a production pricing model. It omits policy exposure details, coverage and deductibles, inflation/trend, censoring, reinsurance, credibility, legal constraints, competitive response, and an approved actuarial rating plan. Synthetic relationships are known by construction, and this exercise does not establish real-world lift. Geography and age are used only to demonstrate segment reporting; their presence is not a recommendation for real pricing. A real deployment would need representative approved data, actuarial review, jurisdiction-specific fairness and compliance review, leakage controls, independent validation, governance, monitoring, and rollback plans.
